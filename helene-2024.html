Hurricanes

Hurricane Helene, September 2024

How a hurricane that came ashore in Florida caused record floods and thousands of landslides in the mountains, 400 miles away.

The story in six steps

Things to notice

    For teachers

    What the 3D view shows
    • Rain columns. Each blue column stands on a town or river gauge. Its height is the rain that had fallen there since 8 am EDT on Tuesday 24 September; its colour shows how hard it was raining in that hour. Values are radar estimates (NOAA MRMS), prepared by NOAA for its Helene in Southern Appalachia dashboard. Heights are exaggerated a great deal so they can be seen: 1 inch of rain is drawn 500 m tall.
    • River gauges. Each gauge is a column of water whose height is the river's stage (its height above a fixed point at that gauge), measured every 15 minutes by the USGS. The red ring marks the NWS "major flood" level; the white ring marks the highest the river had been measured before Helene. 1 ft of river is drawn 120 m tall. Where a column turns grey, the gauge stopped reporting (many were damaged).
    • Landslides. Points from the USGS preliminary landslide inventory. The inventory does not record when each slide happened, so the page shows them all from the morning of 27 September, when most occurred.
    • Storm winds. The white arrows are a sketch of Helene's spin, not measured winds. They turn anticlockwise around the centre and angle slightly inward, and grow longer and brighter closer to the centre, using the storm's strength from the NHC track. Real winds were also steered by other weather systems.
    • Air over the Blue Ridge. The see-through slice in step 3 is a cut through the mountains from the south-east (right) to the north-west (left), drawn on the real ground shape. Dots of air move along it; they are orange while warm and wet, white inside the cloud, and grey once they have lost their water. How much rain falls from the slice follows the real radar rain near the slice in that hour. Whether air blows onto the ridge follows the storm winds sketch (or, before Helene is close, the south-easterly flow the NHC report describes).
    • The storm. The centre follows the NHC best track (positions every 6 hours, joined with straight lines). The cloud spiral is a drawing to show size and position, not a satellite image.
    • Terrain. Mountains are drawn 2× taller than they are by default so the Blue Ridge is easy to see. Use the switch at the top to see true height.
    Limitations
    • Radar rainfall estimates can differ from rain gauges. The highest gauge total (30.78 in at Busick) is higher than the radar values at nearby towns.
    • River heights from different sources differ slightly: the USGS provisional peak at Asheville is 24.67 ft, the NHC report gives 24.8 ft and the NOAA crest table 24.82 ft.
    • The landslide inventory is preliminary and misses slides hidden by forest or too small to see from satellites. Some points are at the damage site, not where the slide began.
    • The slope test uses Esri World Elevation at about 40 m detail, so it describes the hillside, not the exact spot.
    • Everything here comes from live services. If a service is down, that layer will say so and the rest of the page still works.
