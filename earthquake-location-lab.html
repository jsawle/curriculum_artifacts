    About Earthquake Location Lab

    Shows how scientists work out where an earthquake started (its epicentre and depth) and when, from the gap between the P-wave and the S-wave at three seismographs: first circles on the map, then spheres underground. Uses ArcGIS 3D (global scene, meshes and 3D lines).

    What is simplified

    • The seismograms are made by the model from the real earthquake's position and time, not recorded. Real ones are messier, and the start of each wave can be hard to spot.
    • Waves travel in straight lines at one speed each. In the real Earth deeper rock is usually faster, so the paths bend; seismologists use travel-time tables built from many earthquakes.
    • The seismographs are placed at the centres of real towns. Real networks have many more seismographs, at other sites.
    • Three seismographs are the fewest that can pin down an earthquake this way. With more, a computer finds the place that fits them all best.

    Some ways it could be used

    • Stepping through it together on a big screen (Page Down and Page Up also move between steps, so a presentation clicker works).
    • Students measuring the gaps themselves in step 4, then seeing how small reading mistakes move the answer, especially the depth of a shallow earthquake such as L'Aquila.
    • The mystery earthquake as a challenge: nobody sees where it is until step 10.
    • Comparing the three real earthquakes: the deeper the start, the more the circles overlap.

    Data sources

    • Earthquakes: USGS earthquake catalogue (time, epicentre, depth, magnitude). L'Aquila 2009: event usp000gvtu (location by INGV). Loma Prieta 1989: nc216859. Nisqually 2001: uw10530748.
    • Background facts: Wikipedia, "2009 L'Aquila earthquake", "1989 Loma Prieta earthquake", "2001 Nisqually earthquake".
    • Town positions for the seismographs: Wikipedia (Rieti, Teramo, Avezzano; San Jose, Hollister, Santa Cruz; Olympia, Bremerton, Eatonville).
    • Method: the S − P method as taught in school activities, for example Oregon State University CEETEP, "Earthquake location", and University of Bergen SEIS-SCHOOL, "Earthquake location".
    • Basemap: Esri World Imagery with World Boundaries and Places; Esri world elevation.
