About Subduction Zones Lab (test)

Test copy: a 3D block lifted out of the Earth above each trench, instead of slicing the whole Earth in half.

Shows what happens where one plate sinks under another: the earthquakes and plate boundaries on the map, then a block of the Earth lifted out above the trench, with the sinking plate fitted through the earthquakes. Uses ArcGIS 3D (global scene, mesh shapes).

Data sources

  • Earthquakes: USGS earthquake catalogue (FDSN event service), magnitude 4.5+, past 5 years, within 1,300 km of the middle of the block. Saved in the app's data folder and refreshed once a month (the date is shown in the Earthquakes section); if the saved file can't load, the app asks USGS directly.
  • Plate boundaries: "Tectonic Plates and Boundaries" (TeachingAtlas_Curator, ArcGIS Online), from Hasterok et al. (2022), Earth-Science Reviews 231, loaded live.
  • Tonga: Horizon Deep position, plate speed (15–24 cm a year) and plate names from Wikipedia, "Tonga Trench"; Hunga Tonga–Hunga Haʻapai position from Wikipedia.
  • Subduction steps: Wikipedia, "Wadati–Benioff zone"; Earle, Physical Geology, chapter 10; WHOI Oceanus. Layer depths as in Earth Structure Lab (PREM, rounded).
  • Block top: Esri World Imagery with World Boundaries and Places (exported for each block). Globe: the same as a basemap.
