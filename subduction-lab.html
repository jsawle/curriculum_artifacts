About Subduction Zones Lab

Shows what happens where one plate sinks under another: the earthquakes on the map, then a slice down through the Earth with the sinking plate drawn through them. Uses ArcGIS 3D (global scene, slice analysis, mesh shapes).

Data sources

  • Earthquakes: USGS earthquake catalogue (FDSN event service), magnitude 4.5+, past 12 months, within 1,000 km of each trench point, loaded live.
  • Tonga: Horizon Deep position, plate speed (15–24 cm a year) and plate names from Wikipedia, "Tonga Trench"; Hunga Tonga–Hunga Haʻapai position from Wikipedia.
  • Japan: plate speed (7.9–9.2 cm a year), plate names and the 2011 earthquake from Wikipedia, "Japan Trench"; 2011 Tōhoku earthquake position, depth (29 km) and magnitude (9.1) from Wikipedia, "2011 Tōhoku earthquake and tsunami"; Zaō position from the map reference in Wikipedia, "Zaō Mountains". The point on the Japan Trench (38.1° N, 143.9° E) and both trench directions are approximate.
  • Subduction steps: Wikipedia, "Wadati–Benioff zone"; Earle, Physical Geology, chapter 10; WHOI Oceanus. Layer depths as in Earth Structure Lab (PREM, rounded).
  • Basemap: Esri World Imagery with World Boundaries and Places. On the sliced globe the same imagery is draped on a sphere (NASA Blue Marble if it can't load).
