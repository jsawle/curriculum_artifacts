About & versions

Version 2 rebuilds the trade network layers from openly licensed sources only. No geometry, text or zone outline comes from the map image used in version 1. Places, colonies and the sites behind each zone come from the Pleiades gazetteer, and the Persian Empire from the Ancient World Mapping Center. The sea lanes are computed against the public domain Natural Earth coastline.

How the layers are made

  • Places: the 77 trading hubs use Pleiades positions and IDs, plus the colonies and founding cities that Pleiades links by "founded by". Mogador is not in Pleiades, so it uses the position of the island. Tanais is left out because Pleiades dates the city to the 3rd century BCE.
  • Foundation links: a sea lane from each founding city to its colony, where Pleiades records the link or its description states it. 56 links, dated in Pleiades to before 330 BCE.
  • Coastal networks: a model, not a record. Each Phoenician-Punic or Greek port is joined to its two nearest ports of the same culture, plus enough links to connect the whole network.
  • Settlement zones: the coast within 40 km of a site, running 25 km inland. A site counts if Pleiades describes it as Phoenician, Punic, Carthaginian or Greek and dates it within 700–350 BCE. The Etruscan zone is land within 20 km of Etruscan sites.
  • Sea lanes keep about 4 km offshore where the sea allows, follow strait centrelines worked out from the coastline, and end at harbours. From v2.1 they are drawn as smooth curves for readability: splines through each lane's turning points, with a gentle arc on long open-water crossings. Each curve was checked to stay 1 km clear of land, otherwise a gentler curve was used. Strait passages and harbour approaches keep their exact course. Checked every 500 m against the basemap at zoom 9: 0.04% of samples fall on land, all at island harbour approaches. The curves are a drawing convention; the exact course of any voyage is indicative.

Known limitations

  • The zones only show where Pleiades describes sites by culture, so coverage is uneven. For example, the Phoenician homeland and Cyprus have few such descriptions.
  • There is no Hallstatt zone. No openly licensed extent was found, so the Hallstatt world is shown by its princely seats (Heuneburg, Mont Lassois).
  • The sea lanes follow today's coastline. Several ancient harbours (Miletus, Ephesus, Utica, Atria, Spina) have silted up since and now lie inland.
  • The overland corridors (Amber Road, Royal Road and others) are indicative lines through a few waypoints.

Sources and attribution

  • Pleiades gazetteer of ancient places, pleiades.stoa.org (CC BY 3.0). Places, colony links and zone evidence sites.
  • Ancient World Mapping Center, AWMC geodata, extent of the Persian Empire (ODC Open Database License 1.0). The Persian Empire layer is a derived database, released under ODbL.
  • Natural Earth 10m land (public domain). Coastline for sea lanes and clipping.
  • Basemaps: Esri World Terrain Base, World Physical Map and World Imagery. The basemap was used only to check the result and to place a few harbour waypoints; no basemap geometry is stored in the data.
